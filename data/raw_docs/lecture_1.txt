# Drag/Drop-diven vs Code-driven
Drag/Drop diven approach:
Power BI and Tableau: These are "Business Intelligence" (BI) tools. They are designed for speed and accessibility, allowing users to create dashboards quickly using a drag-and-drop interface.

# Drag/Drop-diven vs Code-driven
Code diven approach:
Python Libraries (Matplotlib, Seaborn, Plotly): These require writing code, which is more accurate for the Data Science students, visualization happens between data cleaning and machine learning. # Cognitive Load
The total amount of mental effort being used in the audience's working memory to understand a chart.

In Step 3 of our course plan: choosing the most accurate chart so the audience can answer questions in just a few seconds. If the cognitive load is too high, the audience spends too much time trying to process the visual, which means you have chosen the wrong chart.

---

# Cognitive Load
| Load Type | High (Bad Case) | Low/Optimized (Good Case) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extraneous | Using 10 different colors for 10 bars just for "fun." The brain works hard to find a meaning for each color. | Using one single color for all bars so the brain ignores color and focuses on length. |
| Intrinsic | Showing 100 brands on one Column Chart. The labels overlap and it's impossible to read. | Using a Bar Chart and showing only the "Top 10" brands to make the task manageable. |
| Germane | The manager is confused and asks, "What am I looking at?". | The manager immediately says, "I see Apple is leading; let's invest more there!". |

---

# Cognitive Load
The Three Types of Cognitive Load:

| Type | Intrinsic Load | Extraneous Load | Germane Load |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | Effort required to understand the core information itself. | "bad" mental effort caused by poor design choices. It distracts the audience from the answer. | "good" mental effort. It is the energy the brain uses to learn the insight. |
| Example | Comparing the sales of 2 brands (Easy) vs. comparing the sales of 50 brands (Hard). For 50 brands, a Bar Chart manages the intrinsic load better than a Column Chart because it allows for easy scrolling and readable labels. | Using 3D effects on a Column Chart. The audience must mentally "flatten" the image to see where the top of the bar hits the gridline. | A manager looks at your Bar Chart and thinks, "Xiaomi is surprisingly close to Apple in Europe; we need to increase our marketing budget there.”. We want to minimize Extraneous load so that 100% of the audience's brainpower can go toward Germane load. |
( here, the difficulty comes from the content itself, not the design.
Bad effort, This is what we should reduce or remove.

# Cognitive Load
In the context of our Data Visualization course plan , our goal is to achieve the lowest cognitive load as much as possible, so the audience gets the answer in "few seconds".
We must reach the ideal balance:

Intrinsic Load (Low) + Extraneous Load (Dead) +  Germane Load (Low) = Low Cognitive load

Intrinsic → simplify it
Extraneous → remove it
Germane → make it instant

---

# Cognitive Load
Intrinsic Load (Manage it) - appropriate, not necessarily "low.” Some data is naturally complex; Your job is to choose a chart that makes this complexity manageable.

Extraneous Load (Kill it) - as low as possible. This is the "noise" that prevents the audience from seeing the "signal.", you should remove unnecessary 3D effects or distracting backgrounds.

Germane Load (Maximize it): as high as possible. High germane load means the audience is actually "learning"—their brain is working on the insight (e.g., "We are losing money in Europe!") rather than the chart mechanics (e.g., "What does this blue bar represent?").

---

# Cognitive Load

We want to clear the 'trash' (Extraneous) and organize the 'work' (Intrinsic) so that the audience has 100% of their mental energy left for 'understanding' (Germane).”

If the audience spends too much time trying to understand the chart, you have likely forced them to waste their mental energy on Intrinsic and Extraneous Load.
